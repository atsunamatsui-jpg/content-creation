# Setting your rates

Pricing is where most creators lose the most money, and it is almost always by
underpricing usage rights rather than by underpricing the post.

## Starting point if you have no idea

The commonly cited industry heuristic is **roughly 1% of followers as the base
fee for a single in-feed post** — so ~$100 per 10K followers. Treat that as a
floor and a sanity check, not a real price. What actually moves the number:

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Engagement well above category average | Large upward — this is your best argument |
| Niche with high purchase intent (finance, B2B, beauty) | Upward |
| Audience in a high-CPM market the brand wants | Upward |
| Bilingual / hard-to-source audience | Upward — scarcity pricing |
| Usage rights beyond organic | **Often doubles the fee or more** |
| Exclusivity in a category | Upward, and price the opportunity cost |
| Brand wants many deliverables | Downward per unit, upward in total |
| Pure gifting, no fee | Only worth it for portfolio or a logo you want |

## Price these separately — always

Bundling these into one number is the single most expensive habit in creator work.

1. **The content** — your time, production, skill.
2. **The posting** — access to your audience.
3. **The usage rights** — the brand running it on their channels. Organic 30 days
   is a normal inclusion. *Perpetual, worldwide, all channels* is a different
   product and should be priced like one.
4. **Paid amplification / whitelisting** — the brand spending ad money behind your
   handle. This is often worth more than the original post. Price by window.
5. **Exclusivity** — you're turning down that brand's competitors. Price the loss.

## UGC vs. influencer rates

They are different businesses and should be priced differently:

- **UGC (you produce, brand posts):** you're selling production. No audience access.
  Usually lower per asset, but volume-friendly and no audience fatigue.
- **Influencer (you produce and post):** you're selling audience access plus
  production. Should carry a meaningful premium over your UGC rate.

Set `rates.ugc_no_posting` in `creator-profile.yml` deliberately — a lot of
platform campaigns (Billo, Insense, much of Trend/JoinBrands) are actually UGC
deals wearing influencer clothing.

## Raising rates

- Raise on new clients first, not existing ones.
- A results-based raise is easy to defend: "the last campaign did X, so my rate
  for this category is now Y."
- Never retroactively raise inside an agreed campaign.

## Red flags in offers

- "Great exposure" as the compensation.
- Perpetual usage rights inside a single-post fee.
- Payment on performance only, with no floor.
- Rewrites of your caption that make claims you can't substantiate — you carry
  disclosure liability, not just the brand.
- No written agreement on posting dates, usage, and payment terms.

## Disclosure — non-negotiable

Paid, gifted, *and* affiliate content generally requires clear disclosure
(#ad / #sponsored / platform-native paid-partnership label, and affiliate
disclosure for commission links). Rules vary by country — check your local
regulator, and note that the platform's own label often does not by itself
satisfy the law. This protects you, not just the brand.
