# Platform guide

Grouped by the four categories in `README.md`. Follower thresholds, feature sets,
and even company names change often in this industry — **verify before relying on
a specific number here.** The categorization and the "what the form asks" patterns
are the durable part; treat exact figures as a starting point, not gospel.

Replace or extend this with your own findings as you go. The first time you fill in
an application on a platform, write down what it asked in `notes/<platform>.md` —
that is the note that saves you an hour on application #2.

---

## Category 1 — Creator marketplaces (open campaign boards)

These have a browsable list of live brand campaigns you apply to directly. This is
where `templates/campaign-application.md` gets used.

| Platform | Shape of it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspire** (aspire.io) | Large marketplace, brand-run campaigns | Strong for established DTC brands. Profile completeness materially affects what you're shown. |
| **Collabstr** | Listing-style — you post a gig, brands buy | Closer to a marketplace listing than an application. Your title, thumbnail and price do the selling. |
| **Creator.co** | Campaign board + brand matching | Mix of gifted and paid. |
| **Afluencer** | Campaign board, low barrier | Friendly to smaller/nano accounts. High volume, lower average budgets. |
| **Intellifluence** | Bid-style marketplace | Heavy on gifted/product-for-post at the entry level. |
| **Insense** | UGC + paid-ads focus | Often licensing content for the brand's paid ads rather than posting on your channel. Price usage accordingly. |
| **Trend.io** | Curated UGC campaigns | Application-based, tends to be product + fee. |
| **JoinBrands** | UGC / photo / video tasks | Task-marketplace feel, fast turnaround jobs. |
| **Billo** | UGC video for brands' ads | You are producing ad assets, not posting. Use the `ugc_no_posting` rate. |
| **Stack Influence** | Micro-influencer, product-based | Typically product-for-post at scale. |
| **Brandbassador** | Ambassador programs, gamified tasks | Ongoing relationship rather than one-off campaigns. |
| **Skeepers** | Product seeding at scale | Largely gifted. |

**What these application forms almost always ask for:**
- Connected social accounts (they read your real analytics — do not inflate numbers)
- Audience demographics: top countries, age, gender split
- A free-text pitch — this is the box `campaign-application.md` fills
- Your rate, or acceptance of a fixed campaign fee
- Sometimes: 1–3 portfolio links or a prior-work reel
- Shipping address, and whether you'll cover any content costs

## Category 2 — Native platform marketplaces

Free, run by the social platform itself, and the highest-trust signal to brands
because the analytics cannot be faked.

| Platform | Rough eligibility | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok Creator Marketplace** | Follower + recent-activity minimums, varies by region | Brands filter hard by audience geo. Worth doing first if TikTok is your main channel. |
| **YouTube BrandConnect** | Limited availability by region/channel size | Access is not universal — check whether it's live in your market. |
| **Instagram / Meta Creator Marketplace** | Professional (Creator) account, rollout varies | Availability has varied a lot by region. |

**Action:** meeting the bar is most of the work. After that, fill every profile
field — categories, audience, rates — because brand-side search filters on them.

## Category 3 — Brand-side discovery CRMs — **do not cold-apply**

Software that *brands* buy to find and manage creators. There is often no
meaningful creator-side application; you appear because a brand searched and
found you, or because a brand invited you into their program.

GRIN · Upfluence · Captiv8 · Later Influence (formerly Mavrck) · Cohley ·
Fohr · Activate · Popular Pays · IZEA · Social Native · Traackr · CreatorIQ

**What actually works here:**
1. Create the creator profile **if one exists** and connect your analytics — that
   is what makes you appear in searches.
2. Put searchable keywords in your social bio. Brand managers search
   "Tokyo food creator", not your name.
3. Make your email findable in your profile.
4. Then stop. Time spent here beyond setup is better spent on Category 1.

If a brand invites you through one of these, that's a warm lead — respond with
`templates/rate-negotiation.md`, not a cold pitch.

## Category 4 — Affiliate / commission networks

No application to a campaign — you get a link and earn on conversion. Set up once,
earns in the background, and works on content nobody paid you for.

| Network | Best for |
|---|---|
| **LTK** | Fashion/lifestyle; application-based and fairly selective |
| **ShopMy** | Beauty/fashion; commission plus brand gifting relationships |
| **Levanta** | Amazon-focused brand partnerships with better-than-standard rates |
| **Amazon Influencer Program** | Broad; storefront + on-platform commissions |
| **ShareASale / Impact / CJ / Rakuten** | Big general networks; you apply per-brand within them |
| **Mavely / Howl** | Link-based, multi-retailer commission |

**Watch for:** exclusivity clauses that conflict with paid campaigns, and whether
a brand's affiliate terms let you use affiliate links in the same post as a
sponsored deliverable. Some prohibit stacking both.

## Rules that apply everywhere

- **Never inflate follower or engagement numbers.** Every serious platform reads
  your analytics through an official connection. Getting caught is account-ending.
- **Connect analytics wherever offered.** It is usually the single biggest factor
  in how often you surface in brand searches.
- **Complete the profile fully, once.** Half-filled profiles get filtered out of
  searches before a human ever sees them.
- **Read the usage-rights clause before accepting.** "Perpetual worldwide usage
  across all channels" inside a one-post fee is the most common way creators
  underprice themselves by an order of magnitude.
- **Check exclusivity windows** before applying to two competing brands in a
  category — accepting both is a real breach, not a technicality.
